# Spoken Expressions by Situation

!!! abstract "Overview"
    This note collects common spoken expressions for everyday situations and compares how Chinese and English often handle the same social task. The focus is not word-for-word translation, but choosing a tone that fits the relationship, setting, and purpose.

!!! note "学习提示"
    这篇笔记不是让你把中文逐句翻译成英文，而是训练“同一个社交目的，在英语里通常怎么说”。学习时可以先看场景，再记 2 到 3 个最顺口的表达。

## The Shared Logic

Chinese and English speakers often want the same things in conversation:

- to sound friendly
- to avoid sounding too direct in sensitive moments
- to show respect when the relationship is formal
- to keep the conversation moving
- to adjust tone according to closeness and context

The difference is usually not the goal, but the route. Chinese often relies more on context, shared understanding, and indirect hints. English often makes the function of the sentence more explicit, especially in requests, refusals, and workplace communication.

中文交流里，很多意思可以靠语气、关系和上下文补足；英语交流里，尤其是在跨文化或职场场景中，把目的说清楚通常更安全。清楚不等于生硬，只要加上合适的 softener，就能兼顾礼貌和效率。

!!! tip "Think in functions"
    Instead of asking "How do I translate this Chinese sentence?", ask "What am I trying to do here?" Common functions include greeting, softening a request, refusing politely, showing agreement, changing the topic, or ending a conversation.

## Greetings and Openings

| Situation | Chinese habit | Natural English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Casual greeting | 吃了吗？去哪儿？ | Hey, how's it going? |
| First meeting | 你好，很高兴认识你。 | Nice to meet you. |
| Meeting again | 好久不见。 | Long time no see. / It's been a while. |
| Checking in | 最近怎么样？ | How have you been? |
| Opening a message | 在吗？ | Hi, do you have a minute? |

In English, `How are you?` is often a social opening, not a serious medical or emotional question. A short answer is enough:

> I'm good, thanks. How about you?

> Not bad. How's your day going?

!!! warning "About 在吗"
    A direct translation like "Are you there?" can sound like you are urgently checking whether someone is online. In many cases, it is smoother to state the reason directly: "Hi, do you have a minute to look at this?"

中文聊天常用“在吗”先试探，对方回应后再说事。英文消息里更常见的做法是开头问候后直接说明目的，例如 `Hi, do you have a minute to...`，这样对方更容易判断如何回复。

## Making Requests

Requests are one of the biggest areas where Chinese-English differences appear. Chinese may soften a request through tone, relationship, or a phrase like 麻烦你. English often softens it through modal verbs and extra context.

| Direct meaning | Natural English |
| --- | --- |
| 帮我看一下这个。 | Could you take a look at this? |
| 麻烦你发我一下。 | Could you send it to me when you get a chance? |
| 你能不能改一下？ | Would you mind changing this part? |
| 给我一点建议。 | I'd appreciate your feedback on this. |
| 方便的话，帮我确认一下。 | If you have time, could you confirm this for me? |

Useful softeners:

- Could you...?
- Would you mind...?
- When you get a chance...
- If you have time...
- I was wondering if...

这些 softener 的作用类似中文里的“麻烦你”“方便的话”“可以的话”。区别是英文常把这种礼貌直接体现在句子结构里，而不是只靠语气。

Example:

> I was wondering if you could help me check this paragraph when you have time.

This sounds more polite than:

> Please check this paragraph.

`Please` 并不自动等于非常礼貌。`Please check this` 在上下级或任务分配中可以用，但在请别人帮忙时，`Could you...` 往往更自然。

## Thanks and Responses

| Function | Natural English |
| --- | --- |
| 普通感谢 | Thanks. / Thank you. |
| 更真诚 | Thanks a lot. / I really appreciate it. |
| 感谢帮助 | Thanks for your help. |
| 感谢时间 | Thanks for taking the time. |
| 回应感谢 | No problem. / You're welcome. / Happy to help. |

Chinese speakers may sometimes say "辛苦了" after someone helps. English has no single perfect equivalent. Choose by context:

- Thanks for your hard work.
- I really appreciate your effort.
- Thanks for taking care of this.
- Great work on this.

!!! note "Shared principle"
    Both Chinese and English value recognition. The difference is that English often names the specific thing being appreciated: time, help, effort, patience, or work.

中文的“辛苦了”覆盖范围很广，可以表示感谢、认可、体谅。英文里通常要拆开说：感谢时间用 `Thanks for taking the time`，认可工作用 `Great work`，感谢付出用 `I appreciate your effort`。

## Apologies

| Situation | Natural English |
| --- | --- |
| Small mistake | Sorry about that. |
| Being late | Sorry I'm late. |
| Interrupting | Sorry to interrupt. |
| Asking again | Sorry, could you say that again? |
| Formal apology | I apologize for the inconvenience. |
| Taking responsibility | That's my mistake. I'll fix it. |

In English, an apology often works best with a repair action:

> Sorry about the confusion. I'll send the corrected version today.

> That's my mistake. Let me update it right away.

!!! warning "Do not over-apologize"
    Saying "sorry" too often can make normal interaction sound heavier than necessary. For small requests, "Excuse me", "Could you...", or "Thanks for waiting" may be better.

中文里有时会用“不好意思”来开启请求，语气比较轻。英文的 `sorry` 更容易被理解成真正道歉，所以小请求可以优先用 `Excuse me` 或直接用礼貌请求句。

## Refusing Politely

Chinese refusals often use indirect phrases like 不太方便, 再说吧, or 我看看. English also uses indirectness, but a polite refusal usually needs a clearer boundary.

| Chinese idea | Natural English |
| --- | --- |
| 我不太方便。 | I'm afraid I can't make it. |
| 今天不行。 | Today doesn't work for me. |
| 我可能帮不了。 | I don't think I'll be able to help with this. |
| 下次吧。 | Maybe another time. |
| 这个我做不了。 | I don't think I'm the right person for this. |

A complete polite refusal often has three parts:

```text
thanks / reason / alternative
```

英文拒绝常见的平衡是：态度友好，但边界明确。只说 `Maybe` 或 `I'll see` 可能让对方误以为还有机会；如果确实不行，最好给出清楚但简短的理由。

Example:

> Thanks for asking, but I can't make it this Friday. Maybe we can meet next week.

!!! tip "A useful refusal pattern"
    "I'd love to, but..." is friendly and clear. Use it when you want to refuse without sounding cold: "I'd love to, but I already have plans tonight."

## Agreeing and Disagreeing

### Agreeing

- I agree.
- That makes sense.
- Exactly.
- I see your point.
- That's a good idea.

### Soft Disagreement

- I see what you mean, but...
- That's a fair point. I'm just wondering if...
- I'm not sure I agree with that.
- Maybe we could look at it another way.
- I have a slightly different view.

Chinese disagreement may be softened by silence, facial expression, or phrases like 也不是不行. English discussions, especially at work or school, often expect the disagreement to be stated more clearly.

英语里的 soft disagreement 不是“完全不说不同意”，而是先承认对方观点，再提出自己的担心。`I see your point, but...` 和 `I'm wondering if...` 都是很实用的缓冲。

Example:

> I see your point, but I'm not sure this plan will work with our current timeline.

## Asking for Clarification

| Function | Natural English |
| --- | --- |
| 没听清 | Sorry, I didn't catch that. |
| 没听懂 | Could you explain that again? |
| 确认意思 | Do you mean that...? |
| 确认细节 | Just to confirm, are we meeting at 3? |
| 请求举例 | Could you give me an example? |

!!! success "A safe phrase"
    "Just to confirm..." is useful in many settings. It sounds careful rather than confused, and it helps avoid misunderstanding.

`Just to confirm` 很适合邮件、会议、面试、约时间等场景。它的语气不是“我没懂”，而是“我想确认我们理解一致”。

## Small Talk

Small talk is not meaningless. It is a low-pressure way to create comfort before the main topic.

| Topic | Natural English |
| --- | --- |
| Weather | Beautiful day, isn't it? |
| Weekend | How was your weekend? |
| Work or study | How's your project going? |
| Food | Have you tried the coffee here? |
| Travel | How was your trip? |

Chinese small talk may ask about personal details more freely, such as age, salary, relationship status, or family plans. In English-speaking contexts, those topics can feel too private unless the relationship is close.

中文闲聊有时用私人问题表示关心；英文语境里，私人问题更容易被理解成越界。刚认识时先从低风险话题开始，会更自然。

!!! warning "Sensitive topics"
    Be careful with questions about income, weight, age, politics, religion, and relationship status. When unsure, choose neutral topics such as weather, food, study, work, hobbies, or recent events.

## Workplace and School Communication

| Function | Natural English |
| --- | --- |
| 提醒 | Just a reminder that... |
| 跟进 | I wanted to follow up on... |
| 提出建议 | Maybe we could... |
| 请求反馈 | Could you give me some feedback on...? |
| 确认安排 | Just to confirm, the meeting is at... |
| 说明进度 | I'm still working on it. |
| 表示会处理 | I'll take care of it. |

Example:

> Just a reminder that the deadline is this Friday.

> I wanted to follow up on the document I sent yesterday.

> Could you give me some feedback on the introduction?

Compared with Chinese, English workplace messages often prefer a clearer subject, a direct purpose, and a polite closing. Being clear is not the same as being rude.

职场英语里，`I wanted to follow up on...` 比单独发 `Any update?` 更柔和；`Just a reminder that...` 比直接催促更稳妥。核心是让对方知道你在跟进什么，同时不给人压迫感。

## Ending a Conversation

| Situation | Natural English |
| --- | --- |
| Casual goodbye | See you. / Talk to you later. |
| Leaving first | I should get going. |
| Ending a call | I'll let you go. |
| Ending politely | It was nice talking to you. |
| Ending a message | Thanks again. / Best, ... |

Example:

> I should get going, but it was nice talking to you.

> I'll let you go. Thanks again for your help.

## Common Translation Traps

| Chinese expression | Avoid | Better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 你先忙 | You busy first. | I'll let you get back to work. |
| 随便 | Whatever. | Anything works for me. / I'm fine with either. |
| 我知道了 | I know. | Got it. / I see. |
| 加油 | Add oil. | Good luck. / You got this. / Keep going. |
| 辛苦了 | You are tired. | Thanks for your hard work. |
| 我请你吃饭 | I invite you to eat. | Let me treat you to dinner. |
| 慢慢来 | Slowly come. | Take your time. |

!!! danger "The word 'whatever'"
    `Whatever` can sound dismissive or annoyed. If you mean 随便 in a friendly way, say "Anything works for me", "Either is fine", or "I'm good with both."

这些陷阱的共同点是：中文表达背后有一层语境和态度，直译后英文只剩字面意思。遇到这类句子时，优先翻译“我想表达的态度”，而不是翻译每个词。

## A Simple Way to Choose Expressions

Before speaking, ask three questions:

1. Who am I talking to: friend, classmate, teacher, colleague, or stranger?
2. What is my purpose: greet, request, refuse, thank, apologize, clarify, or close?
3. How much pressure does the sentence create for the other person?

!!! success "Core idea"
    Good spoken English is not just correct grammar. It is the ability to choose a sentence that matches the situation, the relationship, and the emotional weight of the moment.
